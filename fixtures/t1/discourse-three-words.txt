Title: Three Gospel Words
Preacher: Fixture Preacher

This fixture is original test copy. It has no markdown headings. It signals
movements the way many oral sermons do: first, second, finally. The T1
chapterer should split on those discourse markers and record character
offsets.

We are looking at a short stretch of Romans that the Reformers treated as a
doorway into the gospel. Paul is not stacking slogans. He is naming the
rescue God has accomplished in Christ and the status that rescue confers.
Before the three words, remember the problem: all have sinned and fall short
of the glory of God. The need is not cosmetic. The need is redemption.

First, we need redemption. Redemption is an old marketplace word. It means a
price is paid to buy someone out of bondage. Paul is not talking only about
money. He is talking about guilt and the false masters we serve to quiet that
guilt. Career, romance, reputation — good gifts become slaveholders when they
have to justify our existence. If a boss can fire you, that is one kind of
power. If a master can beat you without remainder, that is another. Many of
us are polite on Sunday and still owned by something that has no boundaries.
Redemption says someone from outside has paid the debt we could not pay.

Second, God presented Christ as a propitiation. Older English used that word;
newer translations say sacrifice of atonement. The point is the same. God’s
settled opposition to evil is not crankiness. Love that will not be angry at
what destroys the beloved is indifference, and indifference is not holy.
Forgiveness always costs someone. Either the wrongdoer pays or the injured
party bears the loss. At the cross God does not send a third party to soothe
an irritable deity. God himself, in the Son, bears the cost. That is why the
grammar matters: God presented Christ. The Trinity keeps this from becoming
pagan appeasement.

Finally, the result is justification. Forgiveness says you may go; you are no
longer liable. Justification says you may come; you are welcome. It is a
status with rights attached, not merely a vacated penalty. Faith does not
earn that status. Faith receives it. Works of the law cannot purchase it.
