Overview

The Architecture of a Teaching Series

MacArthur's Keys to Spiritual Growth is a six-part topical series organized around a single controlling metaphor: spiritual growth is synonymous with glorifying God, and there are specific, concrete practices — keys — that unlock progressive sanctification. The series covers thirteen keys across six sessions, building cumulatively from the master principle (glorifying God) through confession, trust, fruitfulness, praise, obedience, prayer, proclamation, evangelism, moral purity, unity, and spiritual gifts.

The decomposition data reveals a preacher operating almost entirely in logos register (85% of all units), with exposition as the dominant function (43%). This is MacArthur's signature: the teacher-expositor who builds arguments from text, not from emotion. Pathos appears in only 20% of units, nearly always in illustrations. The series is structurally didactic — and unapologetically so.

Analogy is almost absent. This is a preacher who teaches by precedent, not by metaphor.

Theological Landscape

Where MacArthur's Mind Goes

Sanctification dominates — surfacing in nearly half of everything MacArthur says across the series. This is expected for a series about spiritual growth. But the secondary distribution is revealing: the character of God (Theology Proper) and the life of the church (Ecclesiology) are the second and third most frequent themes. MacArthur's argument throughout is that sanctification is not a private project — it is grounded in God's nature and worked out in community.

Rhetorical Register

The Voice Behind the Voice

The numbers confirm what any listener already knows: MacArthur is a logos preacher. Logical argument, exegetical demonstration, and cause-and-effect reasoning account for 85% of his register assignments. Pathos (emotional appeal) appears in 20% of units — primarily in illustrations and conclusions. Narrative register surfaces in 17% of units, mostly when he retells biblical episodes. Doxological moments are rare (6%) — this is a teacher who wants you to understand before you worship.
